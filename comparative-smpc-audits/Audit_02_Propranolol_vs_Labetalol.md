# Comparative SmPC Audit Sample #2


**Target Therapeutics:** Hypertension - Propranolol + Labetalol

**Database Source:** UK Electronic Medicines Compendium (emc)

**Regulatory Authority:** UK MHRA + EMA Standards

**Focus Sections:** Section 4.3 (Contraindications) and Section 4.6 (Fertility, pregnancy and lactation)

  ---

## Executive Summary
This regulatory audit evaluates the comparative labelling and safety precautions (specifically reproductive) between Propranolol and Labetalol, two non-selective beta-blockers used for hypertension.

---

## Comparative Analysis

### 1. Section 4.3: Contraindications

* **Propranolol 10mg SmPC:**
  * Explicitly contradicted when there is history of bronchial asthma or bronchospasm, hypersensitivity to the medication, bradycardia, hypotension, second or third degree heart block and patients prone to hypoglycaemia.
 
* **Labetalol 100mg SmPC:**
  * Explicitly contradicted in patients with asthma or wheezing, hypersensitivity to the medication, bradycardia, second or third degree heart block, hypotension

* **Regulatory Commentary:**
  * Propranolol mentions hypoglycaemia as a contraindication which is not mentioned for Labetalol however in Section 4.4 there is a strong emphasis on hepatic impairment for Labetalol.

---

### 2. Section 4.6: Fertility, Pregnancy and Lactation

* **Propranolol 10mg SmPC:**
  * States it should not be used during pregnancy unless essential.
  * No evidence of teratogenicity but warns beta-blockers can reduce placental perfusion leading to intra uterine foetal death and immature deliveries.
  * States increased risk of cardiac and pulmonary complications in the neonate during the post-natal period.
  * States it is not recommended to breastfeed as medication can pass into breast milk to a variable extent.

* **Labetalol 100mg SmPC:**
  * States it should only be used during the first trimester if potential benefits outweigh risks.
  * No evidence of teratogenicity but warns of perinatal and neonatal distress in rare cases and mentions beta-blockers reduce placental perfusion (notes that Labetalol is also an alpha-1 antagonist so preserves some blood flow).
  * Intrauterine and neonatal deaths have been reported but other drugs and pre-existing conditions were implied to be the cause.
  * Suggest monitoring for 48-72 hours post-delivery for risk mitigation.
  * Breastfeeding not recommended as it can be excreted in breast milk.

* **Regulatory Commentary:**
  * Labetalol's dual alpha-1 and beta-blocker function makes it significantly safer than Propranolol as a treatment for gestational or chronic hypertension and pre-eclampsia during pregnancy.

---

## Clinical & Regulatory Implications

* **Prescribing Medication:** Pharmacists advising pregnant women or new mothers will prefer Labetalol to Propranolol based on Section 4.6 labelling. Based on Section 4.3 labelling, pharmacists may prefer to prescribe Labetalol to patients prone to hypoglycaemia and Propranolol if hepatic impairment is a concern (based on Section 4.4).
* **Label Claim Specificity:** Labetalol features an explicit claim for hypertension in pregnancy whereas propranolol maintains general claims for managing hypertension
