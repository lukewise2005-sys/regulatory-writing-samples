# Plain Language Summary (PLS) Sample #2

**Trial Title:** Early disease intervention with guselkumab in psoriasis leads to a higher rate of stable complete skin clearance ('clinical super response'): Week 28 results from the ongoing phase IIIb randomized, double-blind, parallel-group, GUIDE study

**PMID:** 37262309

**Target Audience:** Patients, Caregivers and General Public

**Regulatory Framework:** Formatted in accordance with EU Clinical Trials Regulation (EU CTR) Annex V guidelines for lay summaries of clinical trial results.

### What was the goal of this study?

Plaque psoriasis is a long-term inflammatory skin condition that causes painful, itchy, scaly red patches. Guselkumab is a targeted treatment that blocks a specific inflammatory protein, interleukin-23 (IL-23).

The purpose of this phase III GUIDE study was to evaluate if treatment with Guselkumab would lead to clearer skin faster if it was given earlier in the disease, specifically patients who have had moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis for two years or less. 

---

### Who took part in the trial?

The trial followed 880 adults with moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis.
* **41%** of participants were classed as **Short Disease Duration (SDD)** - 2 years or less with psoriasis.
* **59%** of participants were classed as **Long Disease Duration (LDD)** - more than 2 years with psoriasis.

All participants received 100mg injections of Guselkumab at Weeks 0, 4, 12 and 20. Neither patients nor doctors knew which disease duration group each patient belonged to throughout the duration of the study. 

---

### What were the main results at 28 weeks?

Complete skin clearance was evaluated using the **Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI) = 0**. Participants who achieved 100% clear skin at Weeks 20 and 28 were classified as **Clinical Super Responders (SRe)**. 
* **Complete Skin Clearance (PASI = 0) at Week 28:** 51.8% of the SDD group reached 100% clear skin compared to 39.4% in the LDD group.
* **Clinical Super Responders:** 43.7% of the SDD group were classified as SRe compared to 28.1% in the LDD group. 
* **Speed of Clearance:** it took a median of 141 days for patients in the SDD group to reach complete skin clearance compared to 200 days in the LDD group. 

Blood testing showed that Guselkumab had rapidly reduced markers of system wide inflammation across both groups by Week 4. 

---

### What safety information was found? 

**Tolerability:** Guselkumab was well tolerated and efficacy was consistent across both patient groups. 

**Side Effects:** No new or unexpected side effects were identified in the duration of the study.
